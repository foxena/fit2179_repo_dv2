## Part B: Domain Description

### Describe your domain chosen for DV II

My domain is recreational running and ultra-distance running in Australia. The visualisation focuses on the transition from everyday running to a 50 km ultra-distance effort. This is distinct from a sports-watch brand story: the personal training log is used only as a concrete case study for a broader Australian running question.

### Describe the Why and Who aspects of your DV II

The visualisation is designed for an average Australian audience, not specialist coaches or statisticians. The need is explanatory: many people understand running as a familiar activity, but may not understand how training volume, terrain, pace and race distance change what a run means. Following Munzner's What-Why-How framework, the main user task is to compare and interpret rather than perform expert exploration. The story helps viewers see how one 50.15 km run relates to ordinary training behaviour, national participation context and Australian ultra-marathon benchmarks.

### Describe the "What" aspect of your DV II

The data combines several real-world sources. A personal COROS running-watch export was processed into privacy-safe summaries: date, distance, duration, pace, heart rate, elevation gain and derived effort score. Raw GPS traces were excluded. Public comparison data comes from the Kaggle Big Dataset of Ultra-Marathon Running, filtered to Australian events from 2015-2022 and aggregated into 50 km, 100 km and 161 km summaries. AusPlay By Sport and National Data Tables provide running/jogging participation, age, gender, organising-entity and state/territory activity-frequency data. ABS ASGS state/territory boundaries provide the map geometry.

### Describe the "How" aspect of your DV II

The page uses a scrolling storytelling layout rather than a dashboard. It applies course-note principles of visual idiom selection, data-ink ratio, figure-ground, colour, typography and layout. Bar charts support categorical comparison; line and area charts show change over time; scatterplots and bubble plots reveal relationships between distance, pace, elevation, finish time and speed; a calendar heatmap shows routine; and a proportional-symbol map gives geographic context using rates rather than raw counts. Colour is used consistently for emphasis, while muted backgrounds and light gridlines keep the data as the figure. Interactivity is limited to tooltips because the assignment goal is presentation and communication, not an expert analysis tool. The custom effort matrix combines derived heart-rate, distance and climbing-density measures to show effort as more than distance alone.
